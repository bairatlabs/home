Number Rush

Privacy policy

Updated November 1, 2026

Number Rush is a math game for children aged 7 to 9, made by Bairat Labs, LLC (“we”). This policy explains what happens to information when your child plays it.

In short: the game has no accounts, no ads and no tracking, and it never connects to the internet. It keeps its play history only on your device. We see it only if a grown-up chooses to send us the play report.

What the game keeps on your device

  • Progress: which gems your child has found, and which kind of problem comes next.
  • Settings for the game, such as sound.
  • A play log. For each session it records the date, the start time, how long it lasted, whether “Me!” or “A grown-up” was tapped on the “Who started the game?” screen, and how far along the trail it got. For each problem it records the kind of problem, whether it was answered on the first try, roughly how long it took (in ranges such as “under 3 seconds”), and whether Help was used.
  • The date the game was first opened on the device, which the report counts its days from.

The game never asks for or keeps names, ages, photos, voice recordings, location, contacts or any device identifier.

All of this stays in the game’s own storage on your device. It is left out of iCloud and Google backups, so it doesn’t leave the device that way either.

What leaves your device

Nothing, unless a grown-up sends the report. The game doesn’t connect to the internet (on Android it doesn’t even have permission to), and it contains no analytics, advertising or tracking code.

The Grown-ups area opens only after a multiplication question. There, a grown-up can make a picture of the play log and send it with the device’s share sheet. For each session the picture shows the day, date, start time, minutes played, who started it and how many stages were cleared. It contains no names or contact details, and it goes only where the grown-up sends it.

If you take part in a playtest

If you join a Bairat Labs playtest and send us the report, we use it only for that study, as described in the consent form you agreed to. Write to us at any time and we will delete your report.

Test versions

While Number Rush is in testing, you install it through Apple’s TestFlight or Google Play’s testing program. Apple and Google run these under their own privacy policies.

TestFlight shares some information with us, such as the email address you were invited with, crash reports, how many times the game was opened, and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send. On Android, if you have agreed in your device settings to share usage and diagnostics, Google may send us anonymous crash and performance data. We use this only to fix and improve the game.

Children

Number Rush is made for children, and we don’t collect personal information from them: the game sends nothing to us. Sharing the report is behind a question meant for grown-ups.

Deleting information

Deleting the game removes everything it kept. We hold nothing else from the game except reports a grown-up has sent us, and we will delete one on request.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will update the date at the top. If a change affects what happens to your child’s information, we will tell you before it applies.
